## The Set-up
Is it just me, or is the entire offensive security certification scene starting to feel like some kind of Game Freak "Gotta Catch 'Em All' scheme?    We are encouraged by a Hackemon professor (maybe in your University program or some other mentor) to get certs to get into the cyber security field.  After all, certs demonstrate knowledge in a particular cyber security domain in the same way badges demonstrate progress in the Pokemon League.  You have an explosion of certs offered by some massive (and not so massive) vendors.  With the fancy marketing campaign and quite brutal job market, we feel compelled to try and collect all of them.  After all, cert chaser is a thing in the industry.  But it goes further than that.  It really does line-up.  It is a certification pokemon game.  Now withdraw to your favorite browser and follow me as I walk you through how this is a game. 

<br />
## The Sec+
The Sec+ by CompTIA is kind of like the NPC in the beginning of the game that teaches you how to catch a Pokemon.  It's an HR filter and teaches you the "game mechanics."  No one really wants to take it, and we all groan when we have to take it.  "I know, I know" as we spam A (MCQs) so we can get through it as fast as we can.  And why do we take it?  We take it because the game forces you and won't let you progress until it acknowledges that checkmark compliance.

<br />
## Hack The Box 
Hack The Box, with all their recent certification releases, now have 8 certifications as a part of their offerings.  Each certification also comes with a Learning Path to ensure that you are ready for the certification.  So, do I really have to explain how that lines up?  The 8 certifications are similar to the eight badges of the Pokemon league.  You have to collect the different flags in the exam (defeat the individual trainers) and submit the report (beat the gym leader).  The Learning Path is the Route you take to get to the city and is required to be able to take the exam.  The Hack The Box ecosystem even has a badge system in place where you can earn badges.

<br />
## OffSec OSCP/OSCE3
After you sweep all of the badges, er certs, of the Hackemon League, you can step up and challenge the OffSec Four.  The entry level cert into the series is the OSCP.  This is still the industry standard cert to get in the offensive security space.  If you can get through the OSCP, you can challenge the other three certs needed for the OSCE3 stackable certification.  This means you will be grinding the gauntlet back-to-back to squeeze out the OSEP, OSWE, and OSED.  This is just like the Elite Four.  Plus, OffSec also offers training courses and the Proving Ground.  This is obviously an allusion to Victory Road that you have to complete before you can even challenge the Elite Four.

<br />
## OffSec OSEE
So, if you were thinking the OffSec Four were the last certs in the Hackemon League, you would be grossly mistaken.  There is, of course, the OffSec final boss called the Offensive Security Exploit Expert.  This is obviously the champion of the entire league.  A five day grueling, in-person class followed by an intense 72-hour exam.  After, you are tired and beaten down from the gauntlet that is the OffSec Four, you still have your hardest challenge to date.  You still have to muster up all your grit to beat one last boss to become champion of the industry.  But, due to the stress of the grind, very few people do.  It will challenge you to Try Harder to be the very best, like no one ever was.  Hackemon!       

<br />
## Rival Battles
You also have some one-offs that are available to you in the region.  You have the Altered Security's CRTP and ZeroPoint Security's CRTO.  These are very tough battles that are scattered throughout the region.  Even though they are tough, they are not part of the actual Hackemon.  They exist to advance your "Red Team" story arc.  CRTP is the Active Directory NPC that can be challenged after Hack The Box's CAPE Active Directory cert to further your Active Directory story progression.  CRTO can further your OffSec tradecraft story adding the ability to manage your C2 and the boxes you take where OSCP is more about taking the boxes themselves. They are story arcs for the sole purpose of character development.

## Lessons Learned
Sometimes, the more serious people try to make things.  The sillier it can seem.  Relax.  It will be ok.

<br />
## Reflections

My biggest takeaway: We play CTFs.  People get so mad when I say that.  This isn't a game; these are our careers and we are professionals.  We have big competitions where we collect flags, earn points and try to win.  Both can be true.  We are professionals...who play games for training.  Take a deep breath and have fun with the process.  We are supposed to be doing this because we enjoy it.  That is what makes the grind tolerable. Remember what made it special.

What helped me most:

> **Don't take yourself so seriously.  We play CTFs.  Take your career seriously but the path there should be fun.**
