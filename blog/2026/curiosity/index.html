What curiosity looks like

Created in November 23, 2026

2026   ·   career   technical   interviewing   ·   career


Link

  • Blog Set-up


Resumes Fall

First, it was our resumes. They have to be easy to process quick. You have to use action verbs, you have to quantify your achievements, make sure you hit the key search words for the algorithm, make sure you match the resume to the job description because your voice just isn’t good enough. And, we relented and polished our Resumes and ensured that they are squeaky clean with a fresh coat of paint.


LinkedIn Also Goes Down

Then, it was our LinkedIn. They have to be easy for the algorithm to find you so the recruiter can search you like items in a catalogue. Short phrase so they are easily digestible for quick consumption. Flowing so the recruiter can wheel flick and scroll the length and fully consume your essence. Most importantly, it must match, perfectly, to your resume. No one wants to deal with that dissonance of actual human complexity. Just bounce from machine to machine. And, we relented with safely sanitized LinkedIn.


The Portfolio Is Next

Now, I read a post on said aforementioned LinkedIn that suggested that our portfolios should be our 3-5 showcase projects and all of our useful links. Well, I say no more. The line must be drawn here. I draw the line with the portfolio that I can no longer recede on. Our resumes are already ghosts of the visage of who we might be. LinkedIn profiles are just reskinned mirrors for a different medium. Both lost to the corporate rules of engagement.


I Draw The Line

This means my social media rules now have to be thoroughly sanitized for the ease of others using their verbiage and delivering content in their preferred methodology. But, you also don’t get to have my portfolio.

I get one spot on the internet where I can just be professionally me. All the projects that I find important. All the certs, classes, badges, and boxes I completed. Anything, and therefore everything, I deem important enough to share with the world gets space on my portfolio.


The Portfolio Stands Tall

And if the system needs a third outlet of the same prechewed, tasteless scraps of information regurgitated for a third time leaving no quarter for humanity, then, I might not be the candidate for you. My resume and LinkedIn have already been pasteurized of my essence so you can know if I am a good hire. Plus, I am sure there’s a stack of equally polished resumes from qualified candidates. After all, we are all the same.


Lessons Learned

There is importance to maintaining an authenticity in your job applications and interviews. Because at the end of the day, you are the person who is going to show up at the office, the one they are going to have to work next to. So, why try so incredibly hard to convince them that you are one thing, then getting upset when the original you behind the sanitization shows up to the office for work. I understand that we have to play the game with the right resumes and the right ties. But, be the most professional version of you. Not who you think they want you to be. You both (the company and the employee) will be happier if you made the right employment decisions than forcing something that just doesn’t hit.


Reflections

My biggest takeaway: I will add just as many projects as I think worthy for display with joyful glee and reckless abandon. I also accept that this will turn off some recruiters who get overwhelmed at the volume and just click off. That is fine. They have to do what is right for them and their company and I have to do what is right for me. Both decisions are perfectly ok as long as the people making them are comfortable living with the consequences of said decisions.

What helped me most:

Be the most professional version of you, not someone else.
